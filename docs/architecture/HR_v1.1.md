# AURELIUS Human Resources & Team Development Guide

## Purpose

AURELIUS is both a robotics project and a learning platform.

Most team members are still early in their engineering education. Therefore, the goal is not to expect professional-level expertise from day one, but to provide a structured environment where members can learn, experiment, and gradually contribute to increasingly complex robotics systems.

Every member is expected to learn alongside building.

---

# Team Philosophy

## Learn First, Build Second

Nobody is expected to already know:

- Robotics
- Embedded systems
- PCB design
- Control theory
- CAD design
- ROS2
- Artificial Intelligence

The purpose of AURELIUS is to learn these skills through hands-on development.

Mistakes are expected.

Questions are encouraged.

Experimentation is encouraged.

---

## Ownership Over Expertise

Members are not assigned roles because they are experts.

Members are assigned roles because they are willing to learn and take ownership.

Example:

A student who has never designed a PCB can still become the Electronics Lead by committing to learning PCB design and documenting their progress.

---

## Documentation Culture

Whenever a member learns something new, they should document it.

Examples:

- Sensor research notes
- Tutorials followed
- Design decisions
- Test results
- Failures and lessons learned

Future members should be able to learn from previous work.

# Development Roadmap

## Phase 1 – Learning Foundations

### Duration

Month 1–2

### Objective

Learn the fundamental technologies required for robotics.

### Activities

- Linux basics
- Git and GitHub
- ESP32 development
- Electronics fundamentals
- Reading datasheets
- Technical documentation

### Deliverables

Each member should produce:

- Learning notes
- Mini-projects
- Research summaries

Success means:

- Every member understands the basics of their subsystem.

---

## Phase 2 – Individual Experiments

### Duration

Month 2–4

### Objective

Build small projects to validate understanding.

### Example Projects

Power Team:

- Battery monitor

Sensor Team:

- IMU reader

Motion Team:

- Servo controller

Electronics Team:

- Custom sensor board

Controls Team:

- PID simulation

Success means:

- Each member has built at least one working prototype.

---

## Phase 3 – Subsystem Development

### Duration

Month 4–6

### Objective

Combine individual experiments into reusable subsystems.

Examples:

- Power module
- Sensor module
- Motor control module
- Firmware framework

Success means:

- Subsystems can communicate and work together.

---

## Phase 4 – AURELIUS Prototype

### Objective

Integrate all subsystems into the first working robot platform.

By this stage, team members should have enough experience to contribute effectively to a larger system.

---

# Expected Commitment

This project is extracurricular.

Academics always come first.

Recommended contribution:

- 2–5 hours per week during semesters
- More during breaks if available

Consistency is more valuable than intensity.

A member contributing 3 hours every week is more valuable than a member contributing 20 hours once and disappearing.

# Core Principle

AURELIUS is not a competition to see who knows the most.

It is a collaborative effort to learn engineering by building something ambitious together.

The goal is not simply to create a robot.

The goal is to become engineers capable of creating robots.
